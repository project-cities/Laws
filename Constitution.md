# Constitution of a Project City

## **Article I. Founding Principles and Civic Identity**

### **Section 1. Purpose and Mission**

**1.1. Declaration of Intent**
The Project City is founded as a democratic, inclusive, and cooperative urban community, created by and for its residents. It exists to provide a dignified, sustainable, and participatory framework for collective life, and to serve as a living experiment in civic innovation.

The mission of the Project City is to:

* **Enable human flourishing** through just, equitable, and caring urban systems
* **Organize shared life democratically**, with active participation of all residents
* **Model a sustainable urban form** that respects planetary boundaries and future generations
* **Redistribute access to opportunity**, health, education, and meaningful work
* **Foster cultural vitality, solidarity, and civic imagination**
* **Reject extractive, authoritarian, and exclusionary models** of development
* **Encourage pluralism, mutual recognition, and respect across difference**

This city shall not merely be a place to live—it shall be a place to belong, to shape, to co-create.

---

### **Section 2. Legal and Political Status**

**2.1. Autonomy and Legal Recognition**
The Project City seeks the highest level of autonomy possible within its host state(s), consistent with international law and democratic norms. It may operate under:

* **Special administrative zones**
* **Cooperative legal agreements with host municipalities**
* **Public-benefit land trusts**
* **Charters, treaties, or memoranda of understanding**

The Project City shall not claim national sovereignty but may exercise **municipal self-governance**, as permitted by law, to the fullest extent.

**2.2. Charter as Foundational Law**
This Constitution serves as the foundational legal framework of the Project City. All institutions, laws, and practices must be in harmony with its principles. It may be amended by supermajority vote of the Civic Assembly and through deliberative procedures defined in future articles.

**2.3. Relationship to Other Jurisdictions**
The Project City shall maintain open, respectful, and lawful relations with national, regional, and international entities. Where conflicts arise between Project City statutes and external authority, the city shall advocate for peaceful resolution, democratic negotiation, and the defense of its constitutional values.

---

### **Section 3. Membership and Belonging**

**3.1. Civic Membership**
Membership in the Project City is open to all individuals who:

* Reside in the city (physically or through recognized digital residency)
* Commit to uphold its Constitution and shared civic values
* Register with the Civic Registry Office

Membership is **not contingent on citizenship, income, religion, or ethnicity**. It is an act of participation, not inheritance.

**3.2. Rights of Members**
All members have the right to:

* Participate in all democratic processes
* Access public services and civic infrastructure
* Form associations and Civic Block groups
* Propose legislation and civic initiatives
* Challenge violations of constitutional principles
* Receive clear information about governance and finance
* Dissent, protest, and demand accountability

**3.3. Responsibilities of Members**
With membership come civic duties:

* Engage in good-faith participation
* Contribute to collective decision-making
* Care for shared spaces and institutions
* Respect the dignity, safety, and voices of others
* Observe and improve the democratic process
* Participate in mutual aid and emergency solidarity as needed

**3.4. Honorary and Remote Memberships**
The Project City may grant honorary or non-resident civic status to allies, contributors, and diaspora members. These individuals may not vote in territorial matters but may participate in Civic Block groups, digital forums, and co-creative processes.

---

### **Section 4. Civic Values and Ethical Commitments**

**4.1. Democracy as Practice**
Democracy is not merely a method of voting—it is the guiding ethic of this city. Every process and institution must strive for **accessibility, transparency, responsiveness, and deliberation**. Democratic participation shall be facilitated, not obstructed.

**4.2. Pluralism and Respect**
The city shall recognize, welcome, and defend all forms of difference—including ethnic, religious, linguistic, sexual, cultural, neurodiverse, and ideological. It rejects all forms of supremacy, bigotry, and intolerance.

**4.3. Equity and Redistribution**
Social and economic resources must be fairly allocated. The city shall design systems to **redress inherited disadvantage** and **ensure access to the means of life**—including housing, health, education, mobility, and care.

**4.4. Ecological Responsibility**
The Project City shall operate within **planetary boundaries** and prioritize ecological regeneration. It shall:

* Transition to post-carbon infrastructure
* Use renewable materials and closed-loop systems
* Maintain biodiversity and green public space
* Treat the environment as a co-equal stakeholder

**4.5. Transparency and Accountability**
All data, budgets, and decision-making processes must be open to public scrutiny. Citizens must have access to tools that explain how power works and how to change it.

**4.6. Experimentalism and Iteration**
The city embraces the spirit of experimentation. Laws, systems, and technologies shall be viewed as provisional and open to redesign. **Policy should follow evidence, not ideology.**

---

### **Section 5. Foundational Institutions**

**5.1. Civic Assembly**
The Civic Assembly is the supreme deliberative organ of the Project City. It is composed of all members who choose to participate and forms the ultimate site of democratic legitimacy.

**5.2. Civic Block**
The Civic Block ensures that identity-based and professional groups have voice in governance. It supports federation, voluntary association, and deliberative representation beyond geography.

**5.3. Commons Trust**
A public trust shall be established to steward land, natural resources, digital infrastructure, and other commons on behalf of current and future generations.

**5.4. Social Infrastructure Foundation**
An institution tasked with building and maintaining core public goods: housing, transit, health, education, and cultural infrastructure.

**5.5. Conflict Resolution Forum**
A pluralistic and restorative body for mediating disputes, protecting rights, and ensuring that justice is accessible and non-punitive.

---

### **Section 6. The Project City as Movement**

**6.1. Beyond a Single Place**
The Project City is not only a location—it is a **framework** and **movement** for democratic urban futures. Its charter may be adopted, adapted, or federated by other communities around the world.

**6.2. Networked Citizenship**
Residents of different Project Cities may form shared institutions, Civic Block federations, and cooperative ventures. A system of **mutual recognition** of rights and civic identity shall be developed to allow for **inter-city solidarity**.

**6.3. Cultural Mission**
The city shall support the creation and circulation of **art, literature, and media** that imagine better futures, challenge dominant paradigms, and tell the story of the democratic urban movement.

---

### **Section 7. Spirit of the Law**

This Constitution is not a technical manual but a **living document**. It is animated by a belief in dignity, deliberation, and collective agency.

Every clause shall be interpreted in light of:

* The primacy of democracy over hierarchy
* The human over the commodified
* The long-term over the short-term
* The open-ended over the imposed

The goal is not perfection but participation. The city will be made, remade, and made again—through the labor, imagination, and courage of its people.

## **Article II. Rights, Guarantees, and Social Obligations**

### **Section 1. General Provisions**

**2.1.1. Binding Force of Rights**
All rights enumerated in this Article are enforceable obligations of the Project City. These rights are **not aspirational** but constitute **legal, moral, and structural duties** of the city and its institutions. These duties must be met through policies, budget allocations, public services, and democratic accountability.

**2.1.2. Universality and Equal Access**
These rights are guaranteed to **all civic members**, regardless of citizenship, origin, income, race, gender, religion, disability, neurotype, or legal status. No form of exclusion or gatekeeping is permitted in their fulfillment.

**2.1.3. Standing and Recourse**
Any civic member or group may bring a petition or complaint to the **Rights and Equity Tribunal** if they believe a right is being denied, inadequately fulfilled, or systemically violated. Remedies may include financial compensation, public correction, institutional reorganization, or policy revision.

**2.1.4. Evaluation Mechanisms**
Each right must be supported by:

* A **Quantitative Baseline** (initial measurement of fulfillment)
* A **Yearly Progress Target** (improvement or maintenance objective)
* A **10-Year Guarantee Plan** (strategic and budgetary path to full fulfillment)
* A **Fail-Safe Escalation Protocol** (automatic institutional response if progress stalls)

---

### **Section 2. Right to Housing**

**2.2.1. Scope of the Right**
Every civic member has the right to **adequate, safe, and affordable housing**. Housing is not a commodity; it is a **precondition for dignity, stability, and participation** in civic life.

**2.2.2. Adequacy Criteria**
Housing must be:

* Structurally sound and climate-resilient
* Connected to public utilities and green transit
* Free of overcrowding, health hazards, and exploitation
* Sized appropriately for household composition
* Located within 20 minutes of basic services

**2.2.3. Modes of Provision**
The right shall be fulfilled through a **pluralistic ecosystem**:

* Public housing cooperatives
* Nonprofit community land trusts
* Municipal social housing stock
* Regulated private rental options with tenant protections
* Self-built or co-designed housing supported by public funds

**2.2.4. Institutional Duty**
The **Housing and Shelter Commission** shall be the lead agency. It must:

* Maintain a public housing database and needs registry
* Provide universal housing consultation and placement assistance
* Facilitate new units through land allocation, subsidies, and permitting
* Prevent displacement and eviction through early warning systems
* Acquire vacant or speculative properties for public use

**2.2.5. Evaluation & Escalation**

* **Baseline**: % of civic members housed in adequate conditions
* **Target**: 5% increase annually in adequacy and affordability metrics
* **If progress is <50% of target for 2 consecutive years**:

  * Housing Commissioner is placed under review
  * Emergency fund from the **Commons Trust** is automatically activated
  * Civic Assembly may appoint independent Housing Oversight Board
  * Land use regulations may be temporarily relaxed to fast-track builds

---

### **Section 3. Right to Food**

**2.3.1. Definition**
All civic members have the right to **sufficient, nutritious, and culturally appropriate food** at no cost or at a cost relative to income. No person shall experience hunger, food insecurity, or nutritional exclusion.

**2.3.2. Public Infrastructure**
To fulfill this right, the city shall:

* Maintain a **Public Food Distribution System**, including kitchens, pantries, and vouchers
* Establish **Urban and Peri-Urban Agricultural Zones**
* Operate **Nutritional Cooperative Stores** that sell below-market cost
* Regulate food waste and excess for redistribution
* Guarantee free meals in all public institutions (schools, hospitals, shelters)

**2.3.3. Institutional Responsibility**
The **Food Sovereignty Authority**, working with Civic Block agricultural, dietary, and cultural food groups, must:

* Conduct food security mapping
* Create annual plans for supply chain resilience
* Implement a real-time digital food affordability dashboard
* Maintain strategic food reserves and distribution logistics

**2.3.4. Evaluation & Escalation**

* **Baseline**: Food insecurity rate and nutritional sufficiency index
* **Target**: Annual 10% reduction in food insecurity
* **Failure protocol (if unmet for 3 years)**:

  * Redistribution budget automatically increases by 20%
  * Emergency task force with Civic Block representation takes co-control
  * Urban land use is reprioritized for local food production
  * External civic audit is commissioned

---

### **Section 4. Right to Health and Care**

**2.4.1. Definition**
Every person has the right to comprehensive, preventative, and dignified **health and care services**, regardless of age, gender, or legal status. Care includes mental health, elder care, disability support, and reproductive health.

**2.4.2. Health Delivery System**
The Project City shall guarantee:

* **Free or means-adjusted universal health coverage**
* **Neighborhood clinics**, mobile units, and trauma-informed care hubs
* **Mental health cooperatives** with peer-led models
* **Public childcare and eldercare systems** with paid community caregivers
* **Health translation and mediation services** in all public centers

**2.4.3. Care Infrastructure**
A **City of Care Map** must be maintained to ensure every resident is:

* Within 15 minutes of a clinic
* Within reach of basic emotional, maternal, and social care
* Included in community-based health planning efforts

**2.4.4. Evaluation & Escalation**

* **Baseline**: Access index by demographics and districts
* **Target**: Full access within 5 years
* **If gaps persist**:

  * Care budgets auto-increase proportionally in failing zones
  * Health leadership is rotated to community-nominated staff
  * Universal health voucher system is deployed for missing areas

---

### **Section 5. Right to Education**

**2.5.1. Definition**
All residents have the right to **free, pluralistic, and critical education** from early childhood through lifelong learning. Education is to be **anti-oppressive**, creative, and practical.

**2.5.2. Education Model**
The city shall:

* Guarantee public early childhood centers in every neighborhood
* Operate **tuition-free public schools** that reflect cultural and linguistic diversity
* Fund **Civic Learning Institutes** for adults, refugees, and excluded groups
* Include **Ecological, Democratic, and Technological Literacy** in all programs
* Train all educators in **trauma-informed and participatory pedagogy**

**2.5.3. Evaluation & Escalation**

* **Baseline**: Enrollment, retention, graduation, and equity metrics
* **Target**: 100% basic literacy within 5 years; racial and gender parity
* **If unmet**:

  * Local school boards are restructured with community majority
  * Curriculum is re-evaluated by Civic Block educator groups
  * External pedagogy fellowships are mandated for underperforming zones

---

### **Section 6. Right to Work and Economic Security**

**2.6.1. Definition**
All civic members have the right to **meaningful work, fair compensation, and social protection**. No one shall be required to accept demeaning or exploitative labor to survive.

**2.6.2. Guarantee of Work**
The Project City shall offer:

* A **Civic Employment Guarantee**, funded through public investment
* Worker-owned cooperatives and public enterprises
* Job placement, training, and reskilling hubs
* Basic income or unemployment stipends as fallback

**2.6.3. Labor Rights**
Every worker, formal or informal, has the right to:

* Unionize or form professional Civic Block groups
* Refuse unsafe or degrading work
* Receive non-discriminatory wages
* Participate in workplace governance

**2.6.4. Evaluation & Escalation**

* **Baseline**: Underemployment and wage equity indices
* **Target**: 95% economic inclusion within 5 years
* **If unmet**:

  * Civic Employment Fund auto-activates
  * Private labor platforms are temporarily brought under public regulation
  * Emergency cooperatives are launched in impacted sectors

---

### **Section 7. Oversight and Participatory Monitoring**

**2.7.1. Rights Scorecard**
A publicly updated **Citywide Social Rights Scorecard** shall be published quarterly, showing compliance with this Article by neighborhood, demographic, and right type.

**2.7.2. Rights Council**
A permanent **Council on Rights and Social Guarantees**, composed of civic members, Civic Block representatives, independent experts, and city officials, shall:

* Review progress
* Conduct public hearings
* Propose automatic escalations
* Serve as a forum for grievances and audits

**2.7.3. Citizen-Led Budgeting**
A minimum of **30% of the city budget** must be subject to **citizen-led participatory budgeting**, with spending tied to the realization of rights under this Article.

## **Article III. Governance Structure**

The governance of the Project City shall be democratic, transparent, accountable, and pluralistic. Power shall be exercised through participatory institutions designed to reflect both territorial and non-territorial constituencies, ensuring that all residents have voice in shaping collective life.

---

### **Section 1. Civic Assembly**

**1.1. Definition and Role**
The **Civic Assembly** is the supreme deliberative and decision-making body of the Project City. It is composed of all residents who choose to participate, either in person or through verified digital means.

**1.2. Functions**

* Debate and approve major legislation, budgets, and long-term development plans
* Ratify constitutional amendments
* Oversee executive functions and public institutions
* Host general assemblies on key civic issues

**1.3. Operation**

* Convenes regularly (at minimum quarterly)
* Uses a mix of in-person, digital, and hybrid platforms
* Decisions are made by majority vote, with quorum and deliberation standards defined by bylaws

---

### **Section 2. Elected Councils**

**2.1. Neighborhood Councils**
Locally elected bodies representing territorial districts. They have authority over neighborhood-specific services, planning, and development decisions.

**2.2. Sectoral Councils**
Issue-specific councils (e.g., Housing, Health, Education, Ecology) composed of residents with interest or expertise in the domain, elected via open-list voting among members opting into each sector.

**2.3. City Coordination Council**
A coordinating executive body composed of delegates from Neighborhood Councils, Sectoral Councils, and the Civic Block. Responsibilities include:

* Coordinating between different governance organs
* Managing citywide services and implementation of Civic Assembly mandates
* Drafting legislation and proposing strategic plans

**2.4. Checks and Limits**

* All councils are term-limited and subject to recall
* Minutes and budgets must be published publicly
* Councils may not override decisions of the Civic Assembly

---

### **Section 3. The Civic Block**

**3.1. Purpose and Structure**
The **Civic Block** is a federated chamber representing diverse identity-based, professional, and affinity groups within the Project City. It exists to ensure non-territorial forms of civic belonging and voice are recognized in policymaking.

**3.2. Eligibility and Formation of Civic Groups**
To form a recognized Civic Block group:

* A group must define a clear civic identity (e.g., nurses, LGBTQ+ residents, elders, recent immigrants, artists)
* Must have at least **30 verified members**
* Must adopt a public charter and internal democratic process
* Must register with the Civic Registry Office

**3.3. Rights and Responsibilities of Civic Block Groups**

* May elect **1 delegate per 100 active members**, up to a cap of 5
* Delegates serve 1-year renewable terms
* Groups may propose, debate, and amend legislation
* Groups must maintain active deliberation forums and publish meeting summaries

**3.4. Legislative Role**

* Civic Block delegates may submit proposals to the Civic Assembly with at least **3 co-sponsoring groups**
* Proposed laws must be published publicly for review
* Civic Block has **advisory vote** in all council sessions and **deliberative vote** in Civic Assembly sessions

**3.5. Federation and Alliances**

* Civic Block groups may form **federations** across cities or regions
* Federated groups can share resources, propose cross-city policies, and host joint assemblies

---

### **Section 4. Administrative Secretariat**

**4.1. Role**
An appointed administrative body responsible for:

* Implementing policy
* Coordinating services
* Managing data and infrastructure

**4.2. Structure**

* Headed by coordinators elected or nominated by the City Coordination Council
* Staffed by professional civil servants operating under meritocratic and transparent rules

**4.3. Oversight**

* Subject to Civic Assembly audit
* Required to issue quarterly public reports

---

### **Section 5. Justice and Mediation**

**5.1. Community Courts**
Local peer-based justice bodies focused on restorative practices and mediation.

**5.2. Appeals Panel**
An elected panel to review contested decisions and rights violations.

**5.3. Rights Ombudsman**
An independent civic officer empowered to investigate complaints, ensure due process, and monitor the rights of residents, especially vulnerable groups.

---

# **Article IV. Governance and Civic Representation**

## **Section 1. Principles of Governance**

**4.1.1. Democratic Governance**
All governing authority in the Project City emanates from the people. Power shall be exercised transparently, inclusively, and accountably, through structures that balance **representation**, **participation**, and **deliberation**.

**4.1.2. Separation of Powers**
The government shall be structured into **legislative**, **executive**, and **judicial** branches, each with distinct powers and responsibilities. Mutual checks and balances are institutionalized to prevent concentration or abuse of power.

**4.1.3. Subsidiarity and Autonomy**
Decision-making shall be decentralized wherever possible. Neighborhoods, districts, and voluntary Civic Blocks shall retain authority over matters affecting them directly, provided that such authority is exercised within constitutional bounds.

**4.1.4. Plural Representation**
Governance structures shall ensure equitable representation of all city residents, including marginalized and non-majoritarian populations. No group may be disenfranchised on the basis of origin, belief, language, or affiliation.

---

## **Section 2. Branches of Government**

### **4.2.1. Legislative Authority: The Chamber of Representatives**

The **Chamber of Representatives** is the principal lawmaking body of the Project City, composed of delegates elected through **proportional representation** from geographic districts and designated Civic Blocks.

**Composition**:

* Two-thirds of seats are filled by elections in geographic constituencies
* One-third are reserved for Civic Block representatives, selected through federated internal elections

**Powers and Duties**:

* Propose, amend, and adopt legislation
* Approve the city’s annual budget
* Confirm major executive appointments
* Initiate inquiries and commissions
* Ratify treaties (with Civic Assembly concurrence)
* Override executive vetoes with a two-thirds majority

**Mandates**:

* Representatives serve **four-year terms**, renewable once
* Internal rules shall ensure minority voice protections and proportional floor time

---

### **4.2.2. Participatory Authority: The Civic Assembly**

The **Civic Assembly** serves as the permanent platform for **direct, deliberative, and participatory democracy** in the Project City.

**Composition**:

* Citizens randomly selected through **civic lottery** serve as **rotating deliberators**
* Open seats for voluntary participants with verified civic education certification
* Non-voting observers from Civic Block groups, advisory panels, and ombuds institutions

**Mandates**:

* Debate major laws and proposals submitted by the public or institutions
* Hold **participatory votes** on new legislation, reforms, and civic referenda
* Initiate **public inquiries** or priority investigations into public institutions
* Propose **civic resolutions** that direct executive or legislative attention

**Procedures**:

* Meets continuously, with quarterly full sessions and weekly working groups
* Uses trained facilitators, inclusive technologies, and multilingual materials
* Supported by the **Office of Civic Deliberation**, which maintains neutrality

---

### **4.2.3. Executive Authority: The Coordinating Council**

The **Coordinating Council** is the collective executive body of the Project City, replacing the traditional single mayoral figure with a **distributed council model**.

**Composition**:

* One representative from each constitutional Ministry
* A rotating **Presiding Coordinator**, elected by the Council itself every 6 months
* Observers from the Chamber and Civic Assembly may attend Council sessions

**Duties**:

* Implement legislation and manage public administration
* Oversee urban planning, service delivery, and external relations
* Respond to emergencies and enforce legal decisions
* Submit annual policy roadmaps and progress reports to the Civic Assembly

**Checks**:

* Each executive action must be published in an **Open Executive Record**
* All executive agencies must publish **performance metrics** quarterly
* Emergency powers are limited to 60 days without legislative extension

---

## **Section 3. The Civic Block**

### **4.3.1. Purpose and Design**

The **Civic Block** is a federated network of **identity-based**, **professional**, **moral**, and **interest-based** groups that may formally participate in civic governance. It represents the **plurality of the city’s constituencies** beyond geography, ensuring deliberative inclusion of historically underrepresented and emerging communities.

### **4.3.2. Legal Status and Recognition**

Civic Block groups may be recognized as constitutional participants if they:

* Are open to all qualifying individuals who meet defined participatory or professional criteria
* Have a written charter with a mission consistent with the city’s constitutional values
* Maintain democratic internal governance, financial transparency, and regular membership processes
* Are registered in the **Federated Civic Registry**, which reviews status every 3 years

Examples of qualifying Civic Blocks:

* Ethno-cultural or linguistic communities
* Religious or moral congregations
* Trade unions and professional guilds
* Migrant and diasporic associations
* Age-specific or disability advocacy groups
* Scientific, ecological, and research coalitions

---

### **4.3.3. Roles in Governance**

Recognized Civic Blocks may:

* Elect representatives to the **Chamber of Representatives (up to one-third of seats)**
* Participate in **Civic Assembly deliberations** through advisory or observer roles
* Propose legislation and constitutional amendments (with support thresholds)
* Nominate members to public boards, panels, and oversight committees
* File public interest litigation and constitutional challenges
* Receive budget allocations for public education, consultation, or capacity-building
* Form **inter-block coalitions** for shared issues

---

### **4.3.4. Formation and Lifecycle of a Civic Block**

To gain formal recognition, a group must:

1. Submit a **charter and code of conduct**
2. Demonstrate **active membership** (minimum 0.5% of population or 500 individuals)
3. Hold internal democratic elections or appointments
4. Undergo **deliberative review** by the **Civic Accreditation Board** (a rotating panel composed of Assembly delegates, legal scholars, and rotating members of the Rights Tribunal)

The Board may:

* Grant **full Civic Block status**
* Recommend **conditional or observer status**
* Deny with reasoning and a right of appeal

All Civic Blocks must renew accreditation every **three years**, subject to public evaluation, fiscal audits, and adherence to democratic norms.

---

## **Section 4. Transparency, Ethics, and Accountability**

**4.4.1. Civic Integrity Office**
An independent **Civic Integrity Office (CIO)** shall oversee:

* Conflict of interest declarations
* Financial disclosures by public officials
* Anti-corruption investigations
* Enforcement of lobbying transparency rules

The CIO shall maintain a **Public Ethics Register**, updated monthly and accessible to all.

**4.4.2. Recall and Resignation Mechanisms**
Public officials may be recalled by:

* A **citizen initiative** with signatures from 3% of residents
* A **Civic Block petition** (for block-elected representatives)
* A **public ethics investigation** confirming dereliction or misconduct

**4.4.3. Mandatory Resignations and Reassignments**
If an officeholder is responsible for:

* A failure to meet **constitutional benchmarks** (e.g., housing, environmental targets)
* Misuse of power or recurring legal violations
* Inability to pass mandatory audits or public hearings

Then:

* They shall be **temporarily suspended** pending review
* The relevant oversight body may reassign them to **non-authoritative civic service** until resolution
* Repeat violations shall result in **bar from public office** for a renewable 10-year period

---

## **Section 5. Civic Participation Mechanisms**

**4.5.1. Citizen Initiatives**
Any civic member may propose a law or constitutional amendment with signatures from **1.5% of the population**. Once validated, such proposals shall receive **priority Civic Assembly consideration** and may be put to a **citywide participatory vote**.

**4.5.2. Participatory Budgeting**
At least **10% of the city budget** shall be allocated through direct civic participation. Residents may vote on priorities, submit proposals, and oversee implementation.

**4.5.3. Civic Audits and Hearings**
Public institutions shall hold **biannual civic hearings** where they present progress, accept feedback, and respond to public questions. Civic Blocks and citizen panels may request **special audits** on underperformance, delay, or suspected abuse.

**4.5.4. Digital and Multilingual Access**
All civic governance systems must be accessible through a **secure digital platform** and in all official city languages. Civic education resources shall be provided to support full participation.

---

## **Section 6. Transition and Constitutional Supremacy**

**4.6.1. Transitional Institutions**
Until full implementation, interim representative and participatory bodies shall be appointed or elected through **transparent transitional procedures** overseen by the Foundational Council.

**4.6.2. Supremacy of Civic Representation**
No institution, public or private, may override the deliberative and representative decisions of the Project City’s constitutional bodies, except as outlined by this Constitution.

## **Article VIII. International and Interjurisdictional Affairs**

### **Section 1. General Principles**

**8.1.1. Global Civic Responsibility**
The Project City affirms its role as a responsible and autonomous actor in the international sphere, committed to justice, sustainability, human rights, democratic governance, and the peaceful cooperation of peoples. Its international actions shall serve both its residents and the common good of humanity.

**8.1.2. Status of International Law**
Ratified international treaties, conventions, and covenants—whether multilateral or bilateral—shall have the **force of constitutional law**. In the event of conflict between international obligations and domestic legislation, **international law shall prevail**, unless such law violates fundamental human dignity or the principles of this Constitution.

**8.1.3. Subsidiarity and Solidarity**
The Project City’s international actions shall respect the principles of **subsidiarity** (decisions made as close to the affected communities as possible) and **solidarity** (cooperation across borders to uphold universal values). Local-to-local and city-to-city cooperation is encouraged in all areas of public life.

---

### **Section 2. Powers and Responsibilities in Foreign Affairs**

**8.2.1. Institutional Responsibility**
Foreign and interjurisdictional affairs shall be coordinated by the **Ministry of External and Civic Diplomacy**, in collaboration with the **Civic Assembly**, the **Chamber of Representatives**, and relevant Civic Block federations (such as diasporic groups, humanitarian coalitions, or transnational professional associations).

**8.2.2. Scope of Engagement**
The Project City may:

* Enter into **treaties, compacts, and memoranda of understanding** with other cities, states, regions, and international bodies
* Join or withdraw from **intergovernmental and supranational organizations**
* Collaborate in **global commons management**, climate accords, peacekeeping, or humanitarian response
* Create **cross-border institutions** for economic, environmental, or cultural integration
* Host diplomatic missions or city-based UN-style assemblies

**8.2.3. Public Interest and Constitutional Review**
All international engagements must undergo:

* A **Public Interest Assessment**, analyzing benefits, risks, and alignment with constitutional duties
* A **Compatibility Review** by the Constitutional Oversight Tribunal
* A **30-day period of public consultation**, open to individual comments, Civic Block submissions, and participatory forums

---

### **Section 3. Procedure for Ratifying Treaties and Agreements**

**8.3.1. Eligibility and Initiation**
Proposals for international agreements may be initiated by:

* The **Ministry of External and Civic Diplomacy**
* A **qualified proposal** (with 10% support) from the **Chamber of Representatives**
* A **citizen initiative** endorsed by 1.5% of civic members
* A Civic Block assembly with verified professional or moral jurisdiction on the matter

**8.3.2. Ratification Process**
All international agreements must pass through the following stages:

1. **Initial Negotiation and Drafting**, in coordination with foreign counterparts
2. **Constitutional Compatibility Assessment** (binding)
3. **Public Interest Hearing** before a joint session of the Chamber of Representatives and Civic Assembly
4. **Participatory Vote** by the Civic Assembly (simple majority)
5. **Legislative Confirmation** by two-thirds of the Chamber of Representatives
6. **Final Review and Signature** by the Ministry of External and Civic Diplomacy

**8.3.3. Emergency Accords**
In cases of climate emergency, armed conflict, pandemic, or disaster, temporary international agreements may be enacted by executive decree for up to **180 days**, after which full ratification is required.

**8.3.4. Transparency and Translation**
All ratified agreements must be:

* Publicly accessible
* Translated into all official languages of the city
* Accompanied by an **explanatory brief** outlining their implications

---

### **Section 4. Compliance and Enforcement of International Law**

**8.4.1. Constitutional Standing of Treaties**
Upon ratification, international agreements become part of the **Supra-Constitutional Code** and shall bind:

* All public institutions and officials
* The judiciary
* Public enterprises and entities
* Civic Block groups in their related jurisdiction

**8.4.2. Domestic Alignment Mechanism**
A **Treaty Harmonization Office**, under the Ministry of External and Civic Diplomacy, shall:

* Review all new and existing domestic laws for compliance
* Issue binding guidance on required legislative or administrative changes
* Alert the Civic Assembly if material conflicts are found

**8.4.3. Automatic Revision Procedures**
If a domestic law is found to contradict an international obligation:

* The law shall be **provisionally suspended** by the Rights and Equity Tribunal
* The Chamber of Representatives shall have **90 days** to propose a revision
* If no revision is passed, the law shall be automatically **annulled**

---

### **Section 5. International Law Institutions**

**8.5.1. Council on Global Obligations**
The city shall maintain a permanent **Council on Global Obligations (CGO)** composed of:

* International law scholars
* Civic Block foreign affairs representatives
* Judges from the Rights and Equity Tribunal
* Representatives from diasporic and migrant communities
* Delegates from allied jurisdictions

**Mandate of the CGO**:

* Monitor compliance with international law
* Conduct reviews of new agreements
* Recommend strategic alignments with global goals (e.g., climate targets, human rights, digital governance)

**8.5.2. International Law Court of the Project City**
An **International Law Court (ILC-PC)** shall be established to:

* Adjudicate disputes between Project City entities and foreign partners
* Hear cases of treaty violations or global rights breaches
* Issue binding rulings enforceable by domestic courts and agencies
* Accept cases from civic members involving international rights (e.g., refugee protection, labor rights under ILO conventions)

**8.5.3. Dispute Resolution and Reparations**

* The ILC-PC may impose **compensatory, restorative, or corrective measures**
* Parties may opt for mediation facilitated by the **Council on Global Obligations**
* Failure to comply with rulings may trigger public hearings, suspension of privileges, or international countermeasures

---

### **Section 6. Special Domains of International Cooperation**

**8.6.1. Climate and Biodiversity Accords**
The Project City shall proactively join and implement **climate pacts**, marine and land biodiversity conventions, and **global decarbonization agreements**. A **Climate Treaty Dashboard** shall track carbon compliance, ecosystem restoration, and adaptation benchmarks.

**8.6.2. Digital Sovereignty and Internet Rights**
Project City may enter into global digital governance compacts to:

* Protect online human rights
* Share secure civic infrastructure
* Interconnect city-level data commons
* Prevent surveillance capitalism and monopolies

**8.6.3. Migration and Diaspora Cooperation**
The city shall:

* Maintain **open corridors** of support for climate-displaced, war-affected, and stateless persons
* Develop **mutual recognition compacts** for identity, degrees, and worker rights
* Support **diasporic Civic Block formations** that can represent external populations in domestic deliberations

**8.6.4. Peace and Demilitarization Treaties**
The Project City may ratify **city-to-city peace pacts**, join **nuclear-free zone treaties**, and support the **global abolition of standing armies**, redirecting resources to shared ecological and health systems.

---

### **Section 7. Review, Withdrawal, and Termination**

**8.7.1. Periodic Review**
All treaties shall be reviewed every **5 years** by the Council on Global Obligations and the Chamber of Representatives, to assess:

* Continued relevance
* Effectiveness
* Legal coherence
* Civic consent

**8.7.2. Termination Procedures**
Treaties may be terminated by:

* A supermajority vote of the Civic Assembly (75%) and a majority vote of the Chamber
* A Civic Block-led initiative endorsed by 5% of civic members
* Proof of fundamental breach by the counterparty

**8.7.3. Reconciliation Clause**
Whenever treaties are terminated, a **Reconciliation Report** must be issued detailing:

* Efforts made to resolve disputes
* Impacts on civic members
* Proposed alternative frameworks for cooperation
